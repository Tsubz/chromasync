432 Hz vs 440 Hz: Physics, Myths and Studies

Two reference pitches for the note A, a third of a semitone apart
Key point: 432 Hz and 440 Hz are two choices of reference pitch for the note A. Tuning to 432 Hz lowers a whole piece by about a third of a semitone. A few small pilot studies report slightly lower heart rate, better sleep scores or lower cortisol with 432 Hz music, but the evidence is preliminary and has not been replicated in large trials.

1. A tuning, not a magic frequency

Concert pitch defines the frequency of the A above middle C. With A4 = 432 Hz instead of 440 Hz, every note of the piece is 1.8% lower, a shift of about 32 cents, or a third of a semitone. The intervals between notes, and therefore the harmony, are unchanged.

A popular argument is that 432 Hz is "mathematically aligned": with a C of 256 Hz (28), the Pythagorean ratio 27/16 gives exactly 432 Hz. In the equal temperament used by most music, A4 = 432 Hz gives C4 ≈ 256.9 Hz. More importantly, the hertz is a human unit (cycles per second), so a round number in hertz carries no physical or biological meaning on its own. 440 Hz was recommended by an international conference in 1939 and later standardised as ISO 16.

2. What the studies found

StudyParticipantsFinding
Calamassi & Pomponi, 2019Healthy volunteers, double-blind crossover pilotSlightly lower heart rate with 432 Hz music; other measures not clearly different
Calamassi et al., 202012 patients with spinal cord injury, crossover pilotSleep scores improved with 432 Hz (+3.6), not with 440 Hz; stress fell with both, not significantly
Aravena et al., 202042 dental patients, randomizedBoth tunings reduced anxiety equally versus silence; salivary cortisol lower with 432 Hz

These are small, early studies, mostly from the same research group, using different outcomes. They justify curiosity, not firm conclusions.
