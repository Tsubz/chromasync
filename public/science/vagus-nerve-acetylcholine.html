Vagal Nerve Stimulation & Extended Exhalation

The Biochemical Mechanism of Acetylcholine & Sinoatrial Deceleration
Core Discovery: While inhalation transiently gates off vagal motor outflow, prolonged exhalation reopens the "vagal brake", releasing acetylcholine directly onto cardiac muscarinic M2 receptors to decelerate pulse rate and induce parasympathetic sedation.

1. The Respiratory Gate Mechanism

The human vagus nerve (Cranial Nerve X) acts as the central highway of the parasympathetic nervous system. However, vagal efferent traffic to the heart is not continuous—it is tightly gated by central respiratory motor circuitry (Eckberg, 2003).

During inhalation, inspiration motor neurons in the medulla inhibit the nucleus ambiguus, silencing vagal signals and allowing heart rate to rise. During exhalation, this inhibition disappears, and a burst of parasympathetic action potentials fires down the vagal trunk.

2. Acetylcholine and Muscarinic M2 Receptors

When vagal terminals arrive at the sinoatrial (SA) node, they release the neurotransmitter acetylcholine (ACh). Acetylcholine binds to postsynaptic muscarinic M2 receptors, triggering:

  • Activation of inwardly rectifying potassium channels (IK,ACh), hyperpolarizing pacemaker cells.
  • Inhibition of adenylyl cyclase, lowering intracellular cAMP and decelerating cardiac phase-4 depolarization.
  • Immediate drop in heart rate, arterial wall tension, and systemic sympathetic arousal.
