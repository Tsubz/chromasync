1. The Superior Olivary Complex Integration

Unlike acoustic beats (which occur physically in air when two soundwaves collide), binaural beats are purely neurobiological. When carrier frequency $f_1$ enters the left cochlea and $f_2$ enters the right cochlea:

Left Ear: 432.0 Hz ───────► Cochlear Nucleus ──┐ ├──► Superior Olivary Complex Right Ear: 437.5 Hz ───────► Cochlear Nucleus ──┘ (Perceives Δf = 5.5 Hz Theta)

The medial superior olive (MSO) detects the microscopic interaural phase difference and begins firing electrical discharges at the differential frequency $\Delta f = |f_1 - f_2|$.

2. Frequency Following Response (FFR)

Through thalamocortical reverberation, rhythmic sensory stimulation causes large populations of cortical pyramidal neurons to synchronize their firing rates with the driving stimulus—a phenomenon established by Gerald Oster (1973) as the Frequency Following Response.
