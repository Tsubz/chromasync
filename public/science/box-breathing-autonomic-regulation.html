2. Carbon Dioxide Tolerance & Cerebral Perfusion

Under acute stress, rapid shallow breathing drives down arterial partial pressure of carbon dioxide ($PaCO_2$), inducing hypocapnia. Hypocapnia triggers cerebral vasoconstriction, depriving the prefrontal cortex of oxygen and impairing executive control.

The dual 4-second retention pauses in box breathing normalize $PaCO_2$, optimizing the Bohr effect (efficient oxygen release from hemoglobin to neural tissues) and preventing panic-induced hyperarousal.

3. Alpha Wave Entrainment (10.0 Hz)

Neurological evaluations (Balban et al., 2023) show that brief box breathing sessions suppress sympathetic skin conductance while elevating parietal EEG Alpha waves ($8-12\text{ Hz}$). This frequency band corresponds to "relaxed alertness" or flow state.
