Resonance Breathing (~0.1 Hz) & Heart Rate Variability

About 5.5–6 breaths per minute · Chromasync paces 5.5 s in / 5.5 s out
Key point: breathing at about 6 breaths per minute (around 0.1 Hz) lines up the heart-rate swings caused by breathing with the natural rhythm of the blood-pressure reflex. Heart rate variability (HRV) then becomes much larger than during normal breathing. Reviews of HRV biofeedback, which is built on this kind of breathing, report small-to-moderate benefits for stress and mood.

1. Why about 0.1 Hz? The baroreflex delay

The arterial baroreflex keeps blood pressure stable: pressure sensors in the carotid arteries and aortic arch slow the heart when pressure rises and speed it up when pressure falls. The loop reacts with a delay of roughly 5 seconds, so it tends to oscillate on its own about once every 10 seconds. This is the origin of the ~0.1 Hz "Mayer waves" seen in blood pressure (Vaschillo et al., 2006).

f ≈ 1 / (2 × 5 s) ≈ 0.1 Hz ≈ 6 breaths per minute
A ~5 s reflex delay gives a resonance period of ~10 s

2. Breathing at the resonance frequency

At a normal pace (12–18 breaths per minute) breathing already moves the heart rate: it rises on the in-breath and falls on the out-breath (respiratory sinus arrhythmia). When the breathing rate matches the baroreflex resonance, the two rhythms reinforce each other. Heart rate then rises and falls in step with the breath, blood pressure moves in the opposite direction, and the size of the heart-rate swings increases sharply.

The exact resonance frequency differs from person to person, typically between 4.5 and 7 breaths per minute, with an adult average close to 5.5 (Lehrer & Gevirtz, 2014). Chromasync's Resonance Breathing pattern uses 5.5 seconds in and 5.5 seconds out: 5.5 breaths per minute, about 0.09 Hz. A laboratory study found that this exact rate with equal inhale and exhale increases HRV (Lin et al., 2014). Clinicians find each person's own frequency with an HRV sensor; without one, 5.5–6 breaths per minute is a sensible default.

3. What the evidence shows

A 2020 meta-analysis of HRV biofeedback found small-to-moderate improvements in anxiety, depression and several physical and performance outcomes, and described it as a useful complementary approach rather than a stand-alone treatment (Lehrer et al., 2020). In a 2025 comparison, breathing at 6 breaths per minute raised HRV more than box or 4-7-8 breathing, although no single session changed blood pressure or mood (Marchant et al., 2025).

The same study noticed mild over-breathing at 6 breaths per minute. Breathe gently and quietly rather than as deeply as possible, and stop if you feel light-headed.
